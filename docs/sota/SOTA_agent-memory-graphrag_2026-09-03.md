# State of the art, September 2026: long-term memory for LLM agents and graph retrieval, applied to a one-person second brain

Research date: 2026-09-03. Method: 40 web searches, ~95 primary-source fetches (arXiv, GitHub, vendor docs/blogs). Vendor-reported numbers are labelled "vendor". Anything not opened at the source is marked UNVERIFIED. The four target properties are referred to as (a) stable structure, (b) new-vs-retelling, (c) evolution/contradiction/supersession, (d) re-derivation by a newer model.

## A. What to adopt and what to refuse

### A0. The one-paragraph verdict

The 2026 literature has split into two camps that both matter here. The *systems* camp (Mem0, Zep/Graphiti, HyperMem, HeLa-Mem, SYNAPSE, TiMem, Letta) keeps posting LoCoMo scores in the 90s. The *evaluation* camp (MemDelta, MemTrace, MemFail, ForgetEval, "Same Ranking, Different Winner", Anatomy of Agentic Memory, MemoryArena, LoCoMo-Plus, DynamicMem) has shown, with controls, that most of those gains come from the embedding model, the scoring target, the backbone, or benchmark saturation; that verbatim retrieval plus redundancy pruning matches or beats engineered memory; and that the actual unsolved problems are exactly your (b) and (c): knowledge updates, forgetting, provenance, and contradiction handling. The design that survives this evidence is: verbatim sources at the bottom; a typed, bi-temporal claim ledger in the middle; a small, rebuildable synthesis layer on top; hybrid lexical+vector retrieval with an explicit embedding version; and a batch "sleep-time" consolidation pass whose outputs are always regenerable from raw. Graph structure is worth paying for only in the claim ledger (entity/topic/time edges) and the topic hierarchy, not as a whole-corpus GraphRAG index.

### A1. Adopt: three coexisting layers, verbatim at the bottom

- **Keep raw text and quote it.** MemPalace's headline 96.6% Recall@5 on LongMemEval came from "verbatim storage philosophy combined with ChromaDB's default embedding model", not its palace metaphor (critique, 2026-04-23, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21284). TriMem (2026-05-19, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19952) argues fact-only stores "inevitably discard fine-grained details" and keeps raw segments + atomic facts + synthesized profiles. Nano-Memory (2026-04-13, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11628) beats RAPTOR, HippoRAG 2 and A-Mem on LongMemEval-s/m with nothing but turn-level max-activation retrieval and query-driven pruning, at 3–5× fewer tokens. Letta's own benchmark (2025-08-12, https://www.letta.com/blog/benchmarking-ai-agent-memory) got 74.0% LoCoMo with GPT-4o-mini using only grep/search/open over files.
- **Type every atom by provenance and role.** MemIR (2026-05-25, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.25869) names "provenance-role collapse" (what the user said vs what the assistant said vs what was quoted) as a top failure and fixes it with typed atoms; MemGuard (2026-05-27, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28009) reports up to +28.27% reliability and 5.8× fewer retrieved tokens from type-isolated memories. For chat transcripts this is non-negotiable: an LLM's paraphrase of your position must never become a claim attributed to you.
- **Synthesis layer as pages, not just vectors.** The LLM-wiki pattern (gist revised 2026-08, https://gist.github.com/joonan30/cbce305684d079dbe9a3fbaefe4e3959; April-2026 Karpathy origin is UNVERIFIED at source) runs 15,259 PDFs → 16,294 wiki pages in 52 categories plus 459 overviews / 368 concept pages / 550 question pages, with a mandatory "supersede check" at ingest and a lint pass for contradictions and orphans. That is (a) and (d) in one structure: overviews are the "sharpest articulation", concepts are the stable vocabulary, and both are regenerable.

### A2. Adopt: a bi-temporal claim ledger with supersession, never deletion

- Graphiti's model (valid_at / invalid_at / created_at / expired_at; superseded edges are expired, not deleted; Zep docs, https://www.getzep.com/ai-agents/temporal-knowledge-graph/, snippet only) and Mem0g's "update resolver ... marking them as invalid rather than physically removing them" (ECAI 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.19413) are the two production reference points. TOKI (2026-06-04, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06240) formalises this: every baseline that keeps an LLM judge on the write path "admits at least one of three write-time anomalies (replay inconsistency, belief-drift skew, audit erasure)"; typing the four resolution heuristics as bitemporal operators with audit rows removes all three. Effect size on LoCoMo is small (+0.86), which is the point: this is a correctness property, not an accuracy lever.
- Separate **event time from dialogue time**. TSM (2026-01-12, rev. 2026-09-02, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07468) shows up to +12.2 absolute accuracy from building a "semantic timeline rather than a dialogue one" and consolidating durative facts. For positions, store `holds(topic, stance, valid_from, valid_to, superseded_by, evidence_docs)`.
- Why this is the hard part: DynamicMem (2026-06-22, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22877) finds no evaluated system "both keeps facts that stay true and replaces facts that change"; MemoryAgentBench (ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05257) finds current methods "fall short of mastering all four competencies", with selective forgetting the weakest; ForgetEval (2026-06-14, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15903) measures deterministic deletion at 5% on identifier obfuscation and 0% cross-lingual, versus 78–85% intent-aware deletion when an LLM hook sits at mutation time. Put the LLM at the supersede/purge step, with typed operators and audit rows, not in the recall path.

### A3. Adopt: hierarchy only where the evidence says it pays

- Hierarchy wins on *aggregation* and *complex reasoning*, loses on *fact lookup* and *detail-heavy summarisation*. GraphRAG-Bench (ICLR 2026, v3 2026-02-22, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05690) Table 2: on the Novel set, Basic RAG with rerank scores 60.92 on fact retrieval vs MS-GraphRAG 49.29, but MS-GraphRAG 50.93 vs 42.93 on complex reasoning; HippoRAG2 53.38 leads reasoning at ~1,000 tokens/query while MS-GraphRAG global spends ~331,000. On the Medical set MS-GraphRAG's contextual-summarise ROUGE-L is 22.98 vs Basic RAG 78.54. WildGraphBench (2026-02-02, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02053): GraphRAG-global best on multi-fact QA (47.64), NaiveRAG best on section summarisation (F1 15.84) and competitive on single-fact (66.87 vs HippoRAG2 71.51).
- So: build topic/community pages (Clio-style hierarchical cluster descriptions, 2024-12-18, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13678; HyperMem's topic→episode→fact hypergraph reaches 92.73% LoCoMo with GPT-4.1-mini, ACL 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08256) for the "what do I know about X" query, but route fact questions to flat hybrid retrieval over verbatim chunks.
- When merging summaries upward, feed source passages into the merge: recursive merging "can amplify LLM hallucinations" past 100K tokens and context-aware merging consistently beats it (ACL 2025 Findings, https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00977). HiRAG's whole-corpus hierarchical index cost 21.9M tokens (~$7.55 on DeepSeek-V3) for one small domain (EMNLP 2025 Findings, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10150) — affordable once, not per re-index of a growing archive.

### A4. Adopt: hybrid retrieval with a pinned, versioned embedding

- MemDelta (2026-06-29, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29914) shows an embedding swap alone moves LongMemEval-S accuracy by +6.2pp (p = 0.004) and reverses the Mem0-vs-RAG ranking (Mem0 +11pp over MiniLM-RAG, −1.2pp vs cloud-RAG). The LLM-wiki measured BM25 finding the primary paper in 118/120 searches at 0.68 ms vs vector 113/120 at 678 ms (gist above). Use BM25 + one embedding + a reranker; record the embedding model id on every vector.
- For (d): Drift-Adapter (EMNLP 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471) recovers 95–99% of Recall@10/MRR of a full re-embed with a learned Procrustes/affine/MLP map at >100× lower recompute and <10 µs query overhead. That is the migration path when the embedding model changes; the derived text layers are re-derived by the new LLM, the vectors are mapped or lazily rebuilt.

### A5. Adopt: a novelty pipeline that is cheap first and semantic last

- Conversational corpora are ~80% redundant by bytes: byte-exact dedup removed 80.34% in the conversational regime (24.03% enterprise, 0.16% BeIR) with "zero measurable quality regression" (2026-05-10, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09611). Do this first.
- Near-duplicate paraphrase: RETSim (2023-11-28, https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17264) is "significantly more robust and accurate than MinHash and neural text embeddings"; SemHash-LLM (2026-07-02, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01601) cascades projection hashing → attention-weighted MinHash → LLM adjudication at "<1% neural verification cost".
- "What is new here": NovAScore (2024-09-14, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09249) decomposes a document into atomic content units, checks each against an ACU bank of history, weights by salience; 0.626 point-biserial with human novelty on TAP-DLND and 0.920 Pearson on an internal set. This is the right shape for (b): the unit of novelty is the claim, not the document. A running-centroid embedding distance (2026-03-02, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01791) is a cheap corpus-level prior but is "orthogonal to reader quality ratings (r = −0.002)" — use it to rank, not to decide.
- Graphiti's own entity dedup sits at F1 0.674 on a LoCoMo-500 eval (v0.29.1, 2026-05-21, https://github.com/getzep/graphiti/releases): LLM-extracted entity graphs are noisy enough that "retelling" detection must run on claims/ACUs, not on graph nodes.

### A6. Adopt: NLI-style contradiction detection scoped by entity/topic, with a human arbiter for belief revision

- Best small grounding checkers on LLM-AggreFact (https://llm-aggrefact.github.io/, leaderboard shows 2024–2025 models only): Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B 77.4 balanced accuracy, Claude 3.5 Sonnet 77.2, Granite Guardian 3.3 (8B) 76.5, FactCG-DeBERTa-L (0.4B) 75.6, MiniCheck-Flan-T5-L (0.8B) 75.0. No 2026 entries were visible; the README's last news is 2024-09 (https://github.com/Liyan06/MiniCheck). DecomposeRL (2026-05-27, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27858) trains a 7B decomposer with GRPO to 86.3% in-domain / 69.8% out-of-domain balanced accuracy across 11 benchmarks with inspectable question traces — the first decomposition method that does not pay an accuracy tax for traceability.
- Decompose with decontextualisation (DnDScore 2024-12, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13175; Molecular Facts 2024-06, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.20079) but audit the decomposer: "Decomposition-Induced Context-Memory Conflict" (2026-08-11, https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10627) shows decomposers substitute parametric belief for source text; a linear probe detects it at AUC 0.86–0.88 while self-consistency sampling is at chance (0.51) and context-aware decoding is "not deployment-ready".
- Belief revision is not automatable yet. BeliefShift (2026-03-25, https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23848; 2,400 human-annotated trajectories, Krippendorff α = 0.76): GPT-4o tracks legitimate updates (BRA 0.83 under RAG) but resists unjustified drift poorly (DCS 0.61); Claude 3.5 Sonnet is the inverse (DCS 0.81, BRA 0.74); "retrieving a prior belief state does not prevent a model from nudging the user away from it." MemSyco-Bench (2026-07-01, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01071) adds memory-induced sycophancy. "When Facts Change" (ACL 2026 Findings, https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.103/) finds larger models detect temporal conflicts but the reasoning "rarely propagates to their final predictions". Design: the system proposes `supersedes` / `contradicts` / `refines` edges with evidence; the person confirms; unconfirmed edges are retrievable but flagged.

### A7. Adopt: sleep-time consolidation whose outputs are rebuildable from raw

- Sleep-time compute (2025-04-17, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171): ~5× less test-time compute at equal accuracy, up to +13% / +18% on stateful GSM-Symbolic / AIME, 2.5× lower amortised cost on multi-query contexts; "query predictability" predicts the benefit — high for a personal archive whose questions recur. Letta moved this client-side onto git-backed memory files (2026-03-16, https://www.letta.com/blog/our-next-phase/; 2026-04-03, https://www.letta.com/blog/why-memory-isnt-a-plugin), which is the right substrate for (d): every derived page carries the model/prompt/extractor version, so a newer model re-derives by replaying raw → claims → pages. The LLM-wiki stores `text_extractor` / `text_extracted_date` per source for selective re-processing; MemOS's MemCube carries "provenance and versioning" metadata (2025-07-04, https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.03724).
- Cartridges (ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06266; 38.6× less memory, 26.4× throughput, effective context 128k→484k on MTOB) and Cartridges at Scale (2026-06-03, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04557; multi-cartridge +10–31 points over monolithic, matches RAG at 3–4× fewer prompt tokens) are the "load a topic as a KV cache" future — but they require weight access, per-model retraining, and "naively mixing cartridges trained in isolation collapses performance to near chance". Keep the design cartridge-ready (topic bundles of verbatim + claims), do not depend on it.

### A8. Adopt: your own probe set, scored the hard way

Build knowledge-point probes (MemTrace, 2026-06-15, https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17328): each fact probed by age × (current state / earlier state / trajectory) × (evidence present / missing / contradicted). MemTrace's 13-configuration study found "the evidence was retrievable 10 times more often than it was missing" when systems failed — the bottleneck is use of evidence, so probes must include false-premise and trajectory questions. Report the scoring target explicitly ("Same Ranking, Different Winner", 2026-05-22, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24060: target choice changes nDCG on 83.4–94.0% of queries and flips rankings; 71% of relaxed source-linked credit was unjustified on audit). Measure forgetting (ForgetEval) and write-path cost, not just recall.

### A9. Refuse

1. **Choosing by LoCoMo/LongMemEval leaderboard numbers.** LoCoMo is ~26k tokens and "fits comfortably inside modern LLM context windows" (Cognee guide, https://www.cognee.ai/ai-memory-benchmarks); it "does not explicitly score knowledge updates" (Mem0, 2026-05-11, https://mem0.ai/blog/ai-memory-benchmarks-in-2026); Mem0 (vendor) 92.5 and HyperMem 92.73 are within noise of each other; LoCoMo-Plus drops the same models ~20 points (GPT-4o 62.99→41.94; Gemini-2.5-Pro 71.78→45.72; 2026-02-11, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10715). MemoryArena (ICML 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16313): agents "near-saturated" on LoCoMo reach 0.15–0.23 success; best long-context agent 0.19.
2. **Whole-corpus Microsoft-GraphRAG-style indexing as the primary store.** Numbers in A3; additionally the OSS library's last release is v3.1.2 (2025-08-21, https://github.com/microsoft/graphrag/releases) and LazyGraphRAG has still not shipped (discussion #1490, last maintainer statement 2024-12-09, users still asking 2026-07, https://github.com/microsoft/graphrag/discussions/1490).
3. **An LLM judge deciding conflicts on the write path without typed operators and audit rows** (TOKI, A2).
4. **Agentic self-written memory as the system of record.** MemDelta: agent self-memory 42% vs basic retrieval 47% on LongMemEval-S; Mem0 matched cloud RAG on 2 of 6 question types "at 50x the cost".
5. **Extraction-only pipelines (facts without raw).** Backbone dependence is severe: open-weight Qwen-2.5-3B produced 30.38% format errors vs 17.91% for API models, a "silent failure" for long-term stability (Anatomy of Agentic Memory, 2026-02-22, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19320); MemoryOS ran 32+ s/turn.
6. **Relying on 1M-token windows instead of structure.** NoLiMa (table last updated 2025-07-17, https://github.com/adobe-research/NoLiMa): GPT-4.1 base 97.0, effective length 16K, 64.7 at 128K; Gemini 2.5 Flash effective 2K. "Context Length Alone Hurts" (EMNLP 2025 Findings, https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05381): 13.9–85% degradation with perfect retrieval and no distractors. Long context *is* competitive for re-derivation over a curated topic bundle (EvoMemBench, 2026-05-18, https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18421: "long-context models remain highly competitive"; MemDelta: full-context 49.8 vs verbatim RAG 47.2, p = 0.34) — use it there, with recite-then-answer (+up to 4% for GPT-4o on RULER).
7. **Bio-inspired activation graphs as the primary index.** SYNAPSE's LoCoMo F1 40.5 vs Zep 39.7 (2026-01-06, https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.02744) and HeLa-Mem's per-category F1 gains of 2–6 points over MemoryOS (2026-04-18, https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16839) are real but small, on F1 metrics that the scoring-target paper shows are fragile; both are fine as optional rerankers over a verbatim store.
8. **Vendor head-to-heads as evidence.** Cognee's Jan-2026 comparison is 24 HotpotQA questions with Cognee on tuned settings and rivals on defaults, by its own caveats (https://www.cognee.ai/blog/deep-dives/knowledge-graph-memory-benchmarks).

## C. Traps and unverified claims

1. **Score incomparability.** LoCoMo numbers mix F1 (SYNAPSE 40.5, HeLa-Mem ~40–52 per category), LLM-judge (Mem0 66.88 → vendor 92.5; HyperMem 92.73), Recall@k (MemPalace 96.6), different judges, and adversarial-question inclusion (446 of 1,986 often excluded — Cognee guide). The scoring-target paper shows nDCG changes on 83.4–94.0% of queries by target choice alone. Never compare across papers without the protocol.
2. **Backbone and embedding confounds.** MemDelta: +6.2pp from an embedding swap; rankings reverse across model families (Gemini +14pp from full context, Sonnet +31pp from RAG, partly refusals). Anatomy: 30.38% format-error rate on Qwen-2.5-3B. Any number here with a 2024–2025 backbone will move under Opus 5 / Fable 5.1.
3. **Benchmark saturation and contamination.** LoCoMo (~26k tokens) is inside every current window; LongMemEval's cleaned 2025-09 release is not comparable to earlier scores; HotpotQA/MuSiQue are "based on older Wikipedia data" with contamination risk (Cognee guide); temporal-KG evaluation inflates on known facts (Beyond Known Facts). LongMemEval-V2 (115M tokens) and BEAM (10M) are the only unsaturated public scales, and both are far from the chat-transcript regime.
4. **Vendor numbers.** Mem0's 92.5/94.4/64.1/48.6, Zep's "80.32% LoCoMo" (reported by Mem0 about Zep), Cognee's BEAM 0.79/0.67 and 24-question HotpotQA table, and Letta's 74.0 are all self- or competitor-reported. None was reproduced by the evaluation-camp papers above at those levels.
5. **RULER V2** (https://openreview.net/pdf?id=ZU9tRffRSA): the claim of 12 tasks over 8k–128k, RULER-v1 saturation, closed > open, hybrid architectures underperforming, comes from a search snippet; OpenReview blocked fetches and an arXiv title search returned 0 results. UNVERIFIED.
6. **April-2026 long-context numbers** (https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/long-context-retrieval-needle-in-haystack-2026, 2026-04-24: "GPT-5.5", "Gemini 3 Deep Think", "Claude Opus 4.7", "DeepSeek V4-Pro"; single-needle 200K→1M Opus 4.7 99→89; 8-needle DeepSeek 84→41; "MRCR v2"). Secondary blog citing model cards; Opus 4.7 is confirmed to exist by Anthropic's models page but the numbers are UNVERIFIED. NoLiMa's public table has not been updated since 2025-07-17, so there is no verified effective-length number for any 2026 model.
7. **"Context rot published March 2026, 18 models"** (Morph and others). Chroma's own page dates the report 2025-07-14; the March 2026 item is Context-1. Secondary sources conflate them.
8. **A-Mem's 85–93% token reduction** appears in secondary summaries, not the arXiv abstract. UNVERIFIED exact figure.
9. **Karpathy's April-2026 LLM-wiki post**: only secondary write-ups were reachable (one returned 403); the joonan30 gist is the verified implementation. The 16M-views claim is UNVERIFIED.
10. **"Residual-based attention" novelty / surprise detection**: no primary source found in 2026 for text corpora; the closest verified methods are ACU-bank novelty (NovAScore), running-centroid distance, and rate-distortion "decision-relevant" forgetting (DeMem). Treat surprise-from-attention as an open research direction, not a component.
11. **Graph extraction quality is the hidden ceiling.** Graphiti entity F1 0.674 (its own eval), GraphRAG-Bench's 331K-token global queries, HiRAG's 21.9M-token index, DEG-RAG's need to denoise LLM-built graphs, and WildGraphBench's summarisation collapse all point the same way: the graph must be small, typed and over claims you have already verified, or it becomes a second source of contradictions.
12. **Memory sycophancy and drift.** BeliefShift, MemSyco-Bench and "When Facts Change" together imply a second brain that auto-updates positions from new transcripts will drift toward whatever was said last, and a model asked to reconcile will detect but not act. Supersession edges need a human confirmation step and a visible "unconfirmed" state.
13. **Tokenizer and model versioning.** Anthropic's models page states the tokenizer changed with Opus 4.7 (≈555k vs ≈750k words per 1M tokens). Any chunking, budget or "fits in context" logic keyed to token counts must carry the model id; re-derivation under a new model can silently change what fits.
14. **Deprecations in the reference implementations.** Letta deprecated server-side sleep-time agents, `core_memory_replace`, templates and tool rules in 2026-03; Microsoft GraphRAG has had no release since 2025-08; Graphiti changed its dedup and timestamp pipeline in v0.29.0. Pin versions and keep the derived layers rebuildable from raw, which is the only durable answer to (d).
