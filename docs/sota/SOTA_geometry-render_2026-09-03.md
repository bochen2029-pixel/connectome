## A. Recommendation for this system

### A1. Geometry: Lorentz H³ for layout, Poincaré ball for display, Euclidean for retrieval

1. **Keep retrieval Euclidean for now.** The best 2026 evidence for hyperbolic dense retrieval is HypRAG (arXiv 2602.07739, 2026-02-08, v2 2026-06-04): a 149M-parameter, 768-d Lorentz encoder scores 56.41 vs 54.11 task-mean on MTEB against an identically sized Euclidean BERT, and 0.848 vs 0.798 context relevance on RAGBench, with 3x lower ANN latency on MS MARCO (0.09 vs 0.29 ms) and Recall@100 0.750 vs 0.707. That is real but modest, and it is at 149M parameters; no off-the-shelf hyperbolic encoder at the 1B–7B scale of current Euclidean retrievers exists. HyBIRD (arXiv 2606.28336, May/June 2026) concludes that hyperbolic geometry "is most useful as calibrated structure over a dense anchor, rather than as a standalone replacement for dense retrieval". Moreira et al. (WACV 2024, arXiv 2309.10013) show that a fixed-radius Euclidean encoder matches hyperbolic prototypical networks in high dimension, i.e. some reported hyperbolic gains are radius regularisation in disguise. Conclusion: use hyperbolic geometry for the *map* (where the hierarchy is imposed by you and the exponential volume is the feature you want), not yet for the *index*.

2. **Layout coordinates live in the Lorentz (hyperboloid) model of H³, float64 on the CPU.** Lorentz is the numerically safer model for optimisation (Nickel & Kiela 2018, arXiv 1806.03417; HiM, arXiv 2505.18973, 2025-05-25: the Lorentz variant gives "more stable, compact, and hierarchy-preserving embeddings"). Mishne et al. (arXiv 2211.00181, v4 2024-12-24) show both Poincaré and Lorentz hit float limits and propose a Euclidean parametrisation; umap-learn's `output_metric='hyperboloid'` is exactly that trick, parameterising by (x, y) with z = sqrt(1 + |xy|²) so gradient steps can never leave the manifold (docs: https://umap-learn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/embedding_space.html). Project to the Poincaré ball only for display: rho = x_spatial / (1 + x_0), which is the documented hyperboloid-to-disk map generalised to three dimensions.

3. **The "slide rule" is the Poincaré map itself.** Display radius rho = tanh(r/2) is strictly < 1 for every hyperbolic distance r, so adding depth or documents can never overflow the ball; resolution degrades toward the rim as 1/(1 - rho²), which is the log compression the operator wants. Calibrate the per-level radial step from the branching factor: in H³ volume grows as ~e^{2r}, so a step of Δ = ln(b)/2 per hierarchy level gives each level b times the volume of the one above. With b ≈ 10 (Clio's 10/100/1000 hierarchy is the reference point; arXiv 2412.13678, 2024-12-18), Δ ≈ 1.15, and the four levels root/community/document/chunk sit at display radii rho ≈ 0 / 0.52 / 0.82 / 0.94. Chunks below a focus-dependent importance cut go beyond 0.94, i.e. into the rim, where they are drawn as density, not as pickable instances.

4. **Importance → radius, formally.** The network-geometry literature already has the law: in Krioukov et al. (Phys. Rev. E 82:036106, arXiv 1006.5169, 2010-06-26) the radial coordinate encodes expected degree, with degree decaying exponentially in radius, and the angular coordinate encodes similarity; in the popularity-versus-similarity model (Nature 489:537, arXiv 1106.0286) the radius of a node grows as ln(t) with its age and new links minimise hyperbolic distance. D-Mercator (Nat. Commun. 14:7585, 2023; https://github.com/networkgeometry/d-mercator) infers exactly such coordinates for real networks, with radial position a function of degree. HypLoRA (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2410.04010) finds the same structure inside LLMs: frequent, abstract tokens sit near the origin and rare, specific tokens far out, following a power law. HypRAG measures a 20.2% norm increase from the most general (L1) to the most specific (L5) concepts, with Euclidean baselines flat or decreasing. So use, within a level, r_i = r_level − ½ ln(k_i / k̄_level), clipped to ±Δ/2, where k_i is an importance score (PageRank on the chunk kNN graph, or citation count in the operator's own ledgers). Angular direction comes from the Euclidean 3-D embedding (unit-normalised), children constrained to the hyperbolic cone of their parent, which is Munzner's H3 construction (https://graphics.stanford.edu/~munzner/h3/, 1997–98, 300k edges) re-done with today's embeddings.

### A2. Layout pipeline (numbers for 10⁵–10⁶ chunks)

1. Embed chunks (Euclidean encoder). kNN graph via cuVS/NN-descent or pynndescent.
2. Hierarchy: EVōC (PyPI 0.3.1, 2026-03-27) exposes `cluster_layers_`, a multi-resolution clustering built for embedding vectors; Toponymy (PyPI 0.5.3, 2026-08-17; Cohere/OpenAI/Anthropic/HF/llama.cpp backends) names each layer with an LLM. This is the open-source form of the Clio pattern (facet → embed → k-means hierarchy → LLM label). If you prefer graph communities, Leiden on the kNN graph with `initial_membership` warm-start; at 10⁶ nodes a full rerun is seconds, so the incremental Leiden literature (below) matters for ID stability, not runtime.
3. 3-D Euclidean projection: cuML UMAP (v26.08.00, 2026-08-05). NVIDIA's multi-GPU post (page dated 2026-08-18, citing cuVS 25.06) reports 106M × 2048-d vectors in 8 minutes on 8×H100 and 41–74x end-to-end over CPU; a single consumer GPU handles 10⁶ × 768-d in well under a minute (ODSC/NVIDIA: 10M records under a minute, secondary). Use `init=` with the previous coordinates (documented `init` accepts an ndarray: https://umap-learn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api.html) to warm-start every re-fit.
4. Hyperbolic placement as in A1.4 (pure arithmetic, O(n)).
5. Optional force refinement in H³ is not needed for a map whose structure comes from embeddings; if you want edge-driven refinement, d3-force-3d supports `numDimensions(3)` and `fx/fy/fz` pins (https://github.com/vasturiano/d3-force-3d).

### A3. Stability under growth (anchored re-layout)

- **New nodes, immediately:** `UMAP.transform()` (docs: assumes the new data share the training distribution; https://umap-learn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/transform.html). Position them; do not move anything else.
- **Periodic re-fit without reshuffling:** ParametricUMAP with landmarks is the documented mechanism (https://umap-learn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/transform_landmarked_pumap.html): retrain on new data plus ~1% of old points as landmarks with `landmark_loss_weight` ≈ 0.01; the docs report "the embedding space has been kept consistent" with a clean new cluster. AlignedUMAP (https://umap-learn.readthedocs.io/en/latest/aligned_umap_basic_usage.html) is the alternative but its `update()` only looks backward and retains all data, so it suits batch streams, not an ever-growing corpus. ParamRepulsor (NeurIPS 2024, arXiv 2411.15894) warns parametric DR loses local structure and offers hard-negative mining as the fix, so keep the non-parametric fit as the reference and use the parametric model only as the bridge.
- **Register frames:** after any re-fit, orthogonal Procrustes (rotation + uniform scale, no reflection) onto the previous coordinates of shared nodes; then apply hysteresis, moving a node only if its hyperbolic displacement exceeds a threshold (e.g. 0.1 Δ). Procrustes bounds for embedding alignment are formalised in arXiv 2510.13406 (2025-10; abstract not fetched, UNVERIFIED details).
- **Community IDs:** warm-start Leiden with the previous partition and run FastEnsemble-style consensus (PLOS Complex Systems, 2025-10-01: 10 seeds, co-classification threshold 0.8, tested to 3.8M nodes); reassign only nodes whose co-classification with their community drops below 0.8. For a corpus that reaches 10⁷–10⁸ nodes, LD-Leiden (arXiv 2502.18497; 48.77x over warm-started leidenalg at 214M vertices / 3.30B edges, 0.996 modularity ratio) or HIT-Leiden (arXiv 2601.08554; up to five orders of magnitude faster, modularity within 0.01, in production at ByteDance with 10⁵-edge batches) are the algorithms; note that Core-based Hierarchies (KDD 2026, arXiv 2603.05207) argue Leiden "admits exponentially many near-optimal partitions" on sparse KGs and propose deterministic k-core hierarchies, which is worth a trial for the document layer.
- **Time slider:** store creation timestamp per node; the shader hides or fades by a time uniform, so scrubbing is free. Positions never depend on the slider (the anchored layout is computed once per epoch).

### A4. Render stack

- **three.js WebGPURenderer, r185 (published 2026-07-01), TSL shaders.** WebGPU is available in all major browsers (Safari 26 per secondary blogs, Sept 2025; ~85–95% of users per PlayCanvas 2026-06-03 and utsubo 2026-01-21); three.js falls back to its WebGL2 backend automatically. Native Gaussian-splat rendering (GaussianSplatMesh + CountingSort compute passes) was merged for r186 (radiancefields, 2026-08-10) but r186 was not yet tagged on 2026-09-03.
- **Nodes as instanced quads, never `gl.POINTS`:** WebGPU's `point-list` topology has no point size (W3C WebGPU spec, GPUPrimitiveTopology; three.js example `webgpu_instance_points`). Keep Lorentz coordinates in a persistent storage buffer; the vertex shader applies the focus boost (a Möbius translation is a 4×4 Lorentz boost matrix, one uniform) and the Poincaré projection. Re-centring the fisheye therefore costs zero uploads. Beware issue #29966 (2024-11-25): resizing instanced attributes after creation fails in WebGPU, so allocate for growth (e.g. 2x current node count).
- **Budget:** Apple's Embedding Atlas holds 60 fps up to 4M points and 25 fps at 10M+ on an M1 Pro (WebGPU, 2-D, weighted-blended OIT; arXiv 2505.06386); SuperSplat's WebGPU path renders 10M splats at 124 fps on an M4 Max (2026-06-03). A per-frame budget of ~2M instances (all communities + all documents + chunks within the focus region) is comfortably inside these numbers; everything outside the budget is drawn as a density texture (Embedding Atlas pattern) or, experimentally, as a Spark LoD splat cloud (Spark 2.0, 2026-04-14: LoD splat tree, 64K-splat chunks, 500K–2.5M splat budget per device; 2.1.0 accepts plain xyz/rgb PLY).
- **Picking:** GPU ID-buffer picking (render node IDs to an offscreen target, read one pixel); cosmos.gl v3.4.0 (2026-07-27) does exactly this for hover on hundreds of thousands of points. Do not rely on CPU raycasting at 10⁶ instances.
- **Labels:** hierarchical labels that appear by zoom level with map-style de-overlap (Embedding Atlas; datamapplot 0.7.3, 2026-05-31, with Toponymy layers).
- **Do not use:** deck.gl for the 3-D core (WebGPU "not production ready", picking not ported: https://deck.gl/docs/developer-guide/webgpu); 3d-force-graph beyond ~10⁴ nodes (largest bundled example ~4k); Cosmograph as the engine (CC-BY-NC-4.0, and its August 2026 3-D mode is a 2-D-engine product feature); cuGraph ForceAtlas2 for anything 3-D (x, y only).

## C. Traps, conflicts, and unverified claims

1. **Date drift from page fetches.** GitHub's HTML release pages omit the year for current-year releases, and the summariser guessed. The GitHub API settled them: cosmos.gl v3.0.0 is 2026-06-17 (not 2025), three.js r185 is 2026-07-01, cuML 26.08.00 is 2026-08-05, PyGraphistry 0.59.0 is 2026-09-02. The utsubo "what changed in 2026" post (2026-03-22) dates r171 to September 2025; three.js issue #29966 (opened 2024-11-25, milestone r171) shows r171 shipped around December 2024. Treat that blog's release chronology as unreliable.
2. **PDF summaries can be fabricated.** The first summary of the HIT-Leiden PDF listed LiveJournal/Orkut/Twitter/UK datasets and "2–10x" speedups; the extracted text lists dblp-coauthor, yahoo-song, sx-stackoverflow, it-2004, and ByteDance "risk" (201M V / 4.0B E) with "up to five orders of magnitude". Numbers in section B come from the extracted text.
3. **Hyperbolic gains may be regularisation.** arXiv 2309.10013 (WACV 2024) and HyBIRD (2026) both point the same way; HypRAG's Euclidean baseline is a 149M BERT, not a modern 1B+ retriever. Also, HypRAG documents that "naïve averaging tokens in Euclidean space before projecting to hyperbolic space causes representations to collapse toward the origin" — any DIY "project my Euclidean embeddings to the ball" pipeline will hit this unless pooling is done on the manifold.
4. **Numerics.** Poincaré coordinates saturate in float32 near the boundary; Lorentz x_0 = cosh(r) overflows float32 around r ≈ 89 and float64 around r ≈ 710, irrelevant for a 4-level map (r ≈ 5) but relevant if you ever preserve all pairwise distances of a deep tree (arXiv 1804.03329 precision bounds). Keep CPU-side coordinates in float64 and only ship rho < 1 to the GPU.
5. **umap-learn hyperboloid in 3-D.** The documentation example is 2-D. Whether `n_components=3` with `output_metric='hyperboloid'` is supported and well-behaved is UNVERIFIED here; the safe path is the analytic placement in A1.4.
6. **`UMAP.transform()` assumes stationarity**; a corpus whose topic mix drifts will place new nodes badly until the landmark re-fit. AlignedUMAP `update()` "only look[s] backward" and retains all prior data.
7. **LocalMAP is not inside pacmap 0.9.1** despite the README's "will be embedded soon" (PyPI description, 2026-03-02).
8. **The δ-hyperbolicity paper (arXiv 2512.20926) measures protein language models**, not text embeddings. No 2025–2026 primary source measuring δ on sentence/document embeddings at corpus scale was found; measure it yourself (Gromov δ on a 10k sample is cheap).
9. **WebGPU rendering gotchas.** `point-list` has no point size (W3C WebGPU spec, GPUPrimitiveTopology) — instanced quads are mandatory; instanced attribute growth fails (#29966); forgetting `await renderer.init()` renders nothing silently (utsubo 2026-01-21).
10. **deck.gl WebGPU is "not production ready" and picking is not ported** (developer guide, fetched 2026-09-03); the WebGPU layer list is Icon/Line/PointCloud/Scatterplot/Path.
11. **Cosmograph** is CC-BY-NC-4.0 (README 2.5.1); its shader-grid layout has a hard "space size" ceiling (2022 article: several million nodes "might not fit at all"); the August 2026 3-D mode is documented only in the what's-new page and README, no benchmark numbers were found.
12. **cuGraph ForceAtlas2 is 2-D only** (x, y columns; docs 26.08).
13. **Leiden is not unique.** KDD 2026 (arXiv 2603.05207) states sparse KGs admit exponentially many near-optimal partitions. A figure circulating in search summaries ("2550–2584 clusters across 10 seeds, node-level ARI 0.94") could not be traced to a primary source: UNVERIFIED.
14. **Nomic Atlas:** the release-notes URL returned 404; docs describe a 2-D canvas; claims of 3-D maps and "tens of millions" of points come from third-party listicles: UNVERIFIED.
15. **Gaussian splatting for data visualisation:** no primary source found that uses 3DGS as a scatter/density primitive for embeddings; all 2025–2026 splat work (Spark 2.0, SuperSplat, V3DG, arXiv 2603.02887) is scene reconstruction. Treat "splat fog for communities" as an experiment, not SOTA.
16. **HCC+ (arXiv 2608.24971)** is a single-author theory paper with no experiments; its only number is a 6.1x storage factor vs FP16. Useful as a framing for "rim truncation", not as evidence.
17. **NVIDIA multi-GPU UMAP post** is dated 2026-08-18 on the page but cites cuVS 25.06 (June 2025); the numbers are the same either way.
18. **three.js r186** (native splats) was not tagged as of 2026-09-03; the merge is documented by a third-party article (radiancefields, 2026-08-10).
19. **Clio's cost/accuracy figures** ($48.81 per 100k conversations; 94% taxonomy recovery) and the 10/100/1000 hierarchy come from a ZenML summary, not the arXiv abstract: UNVERIFIED at the primary level.
20. **Safari 26 WebGPU (Sept 2025)** and the 85–95% browser-share figures are from vendor blogs (PlayCanvas, utsubo), not from a browser-vendor primary source.
21. **Kumu**: no 2025–2026 primary source found (searches returned unrelated entities); status UNVERIFIED/unchanged.
22. **Hyperbolic t-SNE (2024)** remains 2-D and ~10⁵ points on CPU (45 min for 89,701 points); no 2025–2026 successor scaling hyperbolic DR to 10⁶ was found. For 10⁶ chunks, hyperbolic coordinates must come from an analytic hierarchy placement over a Euclidean projection, not from a hyperbolic optimiser.
